1Overview

Unit One is a full nuclear power campus built as a single GregTech multiblock family. The heart of it is one machine — the Nuclear Plant Foundation main station controller — whose structure is a mandatory 50×50 platform plus a chain of optional pieces you bolt on as you progress:

  • Steam Generator silo (tier 1–5) — the reactor. Fuel rods cascade down 8-segment pillars; only the bottom rod of each pillar burns.
  • The Spine — a 3×3 steam duct with four chirality-tuned Turbine Housings. Ride the swirl for speed, oppose it for efficiency.
  • The Spiral — a 7×7×12 injection chamber. Eight Precision Injectors each fire exactly 1 L of propellant inside a ±2-tick window per steam slug.
  • Turbine Generator — the final rotor gallery that converts the boosted flow into EU through Dynamo hatches.
  • Cooling Towers (up to two) — 17×17×20 hyperboloids that recycle condensate, double passive heat decay and halve the decay-heat demand.
  • Plant Modules (four) — Vent Stack (safety windows halved), Switchyard (lifts the 100 M EU/t grid soft cap), Extraction Derrick (self-mining) and Enrichment Annex (on-site pellet line).

There is exactly one controller in the whole design. The Space Elevator dock-pad architecture — a controller per building, standing on a flush floor marker — was deleted by the point-of-use ruling. Every building on this page is an optional checked piece of the main station, raised by the same single hologram projection and detected on every structure walk. There is nothing to dock and no second controller to place.

The one-projection build flow

The main station controller's hologram builds the entire plant in a single projection, in this order: platform → silo (at the GUI's Build Tier 1–5) → Spine duct → Spiral → Turbine Generator + output manifold → front Cooling Tower → back Cooling Tower → Vent Stack → Switchyard → Extraction Derrick → Enrichment Annex. You swap the hatches into the sockets and the plant is done.

The controller re-validates its structure periodically (every 600 ticks idle / 200 ticks running), because block updates cannot cross the unregistered concrete; a piece added far from the controller is picked up on the next revalidation. A projection never overwrites a standing machine in a plant-module cell — it skips the cell and the module reports that it cannot form.

Survival cost is roughly 40,000 blocks at full buildout — deliberately "insanely expensive once". Creative testing goes through the projector auto-build, piece by piece.

Fully optimized (tier-5 silo, all-naquadah cascade, all-riding Spine, 8 timed Lv 6 injectors, 8 rotor stages) the plant computes ~1.045 billion EU/t, clamped to your summed dynamo capacity.

2Getting Started — The Blueprint

The five-step commissioning sequence. The questbook's "The Blueprint" quest walks the same path 0.17.0.

Testing in creative? The hologram projector previews one piece at a time and is height-capped, so it cannot raise a whole plant. Run /btsbuild [1-5|all] [uranium|mox|thorium|naq] [force] instead: it builds the entire plant where you stand — every hatch and control block fitted, rotors seated, rod ports stocked, a bench clock spinning the turbine with no redstone, and infinite supplies (feedwater, the best coolant in the pack, and Naquadah Propellant Lv 6 in all eight injectors, topped up every tick, with a perfectly timed 1 L shot per accepted cycle) so it climbs to the redline and stays there. /btsclock off hands pacing back to your own redstone clock; /btssupply off hands the plumbing back to you.

Want to see every tier? /btsbuild all raises all five side by side in one go, on a 60-block pitch running to your right (the 50×50 platform plus a 10-block walking corridor), each one complete and running, and tells you where each tier's main station landed and whether it formed. It needs 40 blocks of headroom, so stand at or below y 215. Both forms refuse to bulldoze a site that is more than 15 % solid unless you append force.
Quests missing in an existing world? BetterQuesting reads config/betterquesting/DefaultQuests only when a world is created — an existing save owns its own copy and never re-reads the defaults, so a freshly merged chapter shows up in new worlds and nowhere else. Run /btsquests to see exactly which of our quest ids are in that world's database, and /btsquests install to add our chapter and the Unit One entry quest to it — player progress is snapshotted and restored around the write, no pack quest is touched, and open books resync on the spot.
  1. Craft the main station. The Nuclear Plant Foundation controller is an IV assembler job: Radiation-Proof Machine Casing + 4× IV circuit + dense lead, tungstensteel, tungsten, iridium and rhodium-plated palladium plates + molten platinum. Place it where the plant's front line will run — it ends up embedded in the silo face at ground level, one fixed spot for all five tiers.
  2. Project once. Set Build Tier 1–5 in the controller GUI (a trigger stack of 2–5 overrides it) and hologram-project the controller. One projection lays out everything: the 50×50 platform, the silo, the Spine, the Spiral, the Turbine Generator, both Cooling Tower shells and all four plant modules.
  3. Build the shells. Follow the hologram: floor layer (2,243 Light Concrete + 249 Tungstensteel Reinforced shield pad + 8 flush Solid Steel markers), silo walls and dome, Tungstensteel Turbine Casing ducts, tower hyperboloids.
  4. Fit the hatches. Nothing docks and nothing needs a second controller. Every socket is on the building it serves: silo face (1 Maintenance, 1 Feedwater Input Hatch, 1 Silo Control Block, 1 Output Hatch, 1 Rod Input Bus, 1 Depleted Output Bus), Spiral (8 Precision Injectors, 1 Spine Sensor, 1 Coolant + 1 Propellant Input Hatch, 1 Maintenance), output manifold back face (4 Dynamos, 4 Energy, 4 Laser Sources, 1 Turbine Control Block, 1 Maintenance), Extraction Derrick (1 Output Bus), Enrichment Annex (1 Input Bus, 1 Input Hatch, 1 Output Bus). The Cooling Towers, the Vent Stack and the Switchyard take none at all.
  5. Fuel and start. Drop fuel rods into the Rod Loading Ports at the top of each pillar (hopper / AE2 / right-click), feed water to the silo, set the control-rod throttle (1–24; a programmed circuit in the controller slot overrides it), pipe propellant to the injectors on the Spine Sensor's clock, keep coolant in the Spiral's input hatch, and enable the machine.
Questbook: the entry quest with item requirements + achievement in the GTNH power-progression chapter, and the BLUEPRINTS chapter listing every structure's items as optional quests (controllers are the required ones), are shipped in 0.17.0.

The rod cascade (Stage 0)

Fuel columns are working gravity pillars, 8 segments tall:

  • Insert at top: rods go into the Rod Loading Port (4-slot buffer; hopper / AE2 / right-click insert). A rod falls to the lowest empty segment.
  • Burn at bottom: only the bottom segment burns. One decay cycle consumes it into a depleted rod, which drops to the base extraction buffer and exits via the output bus. Everything above shifts down one.
  • Live columns: a column is live while its bottom segment holds a burning rod. Partial pillars = partial output; a starved pillar simply goes dark. The automation challenge is keeping 45–201 pillars topped up and drained.
  • Decay cycle: one eighth of the classic campaign — at the default 720-minute campaign, one cycle burns the bottom rod every 90 real-time minutes.
Steam L/t = live columns × 16,384 × rod multiplier × N/24 (N = control-rod throttle)
Assembly fallback (heavy penalty): classic fuel assemblies in the input bus still run, at 50% output — the GUI labels it "Assembly fallback — output halved". One bus batch runs 4 half-rate decay cycles. Buffered rods win the plant back the moment they land.
Breaking a fueled pillar frame ejects its rods at the controller. No Turbine Hall / Turbine Generator docked? The silo exports Steam directly — or Hot Coolant when fed IC2 Coolant (tier 5 doubles the coolant flow).

Injection timing — the ±2-tick window

  • Slugs: steam moves in slugs, one entering every 20 ticks while the reactor runs.
  • Sensor: the Spine Sensor emits a full-strength 2-tick redstone pulse (all sides) the tick a slug enters. Lock your timing circuit to this signal.
  • The window: each injector must receive EXACTLY 1 L of propellant within ±2 ticks of the pulse. Every litre is tick-stamped on arrival (16 L capacity — it is a timing instrument, not a tank).
  • Scoring: the slug is scored one tick after the window closes, so timing never depends on tile-entity order; stale stamps from stops are discarded.
  • Misses: out-of-window litres, or a second litre in the same window, are wasted and spike the chamber +50 heat each (capped at 8 per slug).
boost = 1 + (P − 1) × n / 8 n = injectors timed this slug; P = LOWEST propellant among them V2 = V1 × boost all eight timed = the full factor P

Coolant — the load, the interlock, the trip

load/tick = 200 × (RPM/1000)² + |S|² × 5 / 20 RPM term + the Spine's residual swirl (q_spine) coolant L/t = ceil(load / Q) Q = coolant quality (see §4) interlock : a cycle is only ACCEPTED while the chamber can already cover the RPM that cycle would reach — otherwise the RPM is held, and why trip : coolant lost at speed → RPM ×0.95/tick spin-down (not a seizure)
Nothing seizes because you have not plumbed it yet. An unsupplied plant simply never accelerates: no coolant headroom (or dry injectors) means the cycle is refused before any steam or propellant is spent, and every panel prints the reason. A cooled chamber also carries the Spine's residual swirl away, so all-riding patterns cost coolant instead of inevitably cooking the core — a starved or missing chamber takes that turbulence as |S|² × 5 heat per second as before. Seizure survives only as the end of a chamber that was already near SCRAM when its coolant failed at speed.

Output (as implemented, 0.16.0)

EU/t = steam L/t × speed × eff × (1 + 0.75 × stages) / 2 clamped to summed dynamo capacity (GT detonates past it) and < 2³¹

The Turbine Generator takes precedence over a docked Turbine Hall. Fully optimized T5 (201 naquadah pillars, all-riding spine, redline RPM, 8 fresh rotors) peaks ~1.10B EU/t and averages ~1.05B across the decay sawtooth — on the settled ~1B ceiling, above the retired hall's 991M.

RPM model 0.17.0

Cycles become redstone-triggered: a rising edge on the Turbine Control Block starts a cycle — consuming one slug of buffered steam (20 × L/t), opening the ±2t injector window and burning the timed litres. Fan RPM is the central variable:

RPM gain = 176 × P × (timed / 8) RPM decays 0.5% per tick toward 0 (redline 12,000) Equilibrium at 1 cycle/second, all 8 timed: Lv 6 ~11,996 (redline) | Lv 3 ~9,227 | Benzene ~2,307 EU/t = L/t × eff × (RPM / 1000) × avgRotorFactor × stages / 8 coolant = ceil((200 × (RPM/1000)² + |S|²×5/20) / Q) per tick
  • Higher pulse frequency = more propellant per minute = higher sustained RPM. Pulsing faster than steam production = missed cycles (propellant still burned, no gain).
  • Interlock: a cycle is refused outright (no steam, no propellant, no heat) when the chamber cannot cool the RPM it would reach, or the injectors have been dry for 5 s. The turbine holds its RPM and the GUI, both control blocks and the scanner all state the reason.
  • No redstone clock = RPM decays to zero = no output. The Spine Sensor echoes each accepted cycle as its confirmation pulse.
  • Rotors wear like plane turbines: each second, damage chance proportional to RPM/redline (hours at full tilt). Every chip hurts — stage factor 1.0 minus 5% per damage point; at 10 the rotor shatters (item destroyed, stage dead until replaced).

How a module is detected

By the ordinary structure walk, exactly like the Cooling Towers. checkMachine checks each module at its fixed offset and keeps the result in that module's own error list, so nothing a module reports can un-form the plant. A module that simply is not there is not an error. A module with blocks standing that will not form prints its reason on the ungated Plant modules line, which every readout carries in every state — including on a bare platform, which is exactly when these buildings go up. Because module placement uses only compile-time constants, the client-side hologram draws all four correctly, and every module field the GUI reads is a synced value.

Total module buildout: 1,215 blocks (313 + 351 + 244 + 307). Skipping the Vent Stack leaves both safety windows at full length; skipping the Derrick or the Annex costs you the fuel cycle and nothing else, since neither is ever a structure demand and neither can stall the plant. Skipping the Switchyard is the expensive one — see below.

The Switchyard soft cap

Without a Switchyard the plant injects at most 100,000,000 EU/t. The reactor still derives its full figure — the derivation on the MATH panel is untouched — and everything above the cap is discarded. On a tuned tier-5 plant that is the overwhelming majority of the output, so on a big plant this building is not decoration.

It is impossible to hit silently. The OUTPUT headline changes from [CLAMPED from N] to [GRID-CAPPED from N — no Switchyard]; the Switchyard line prints all four states (cap disabled in config / yard standing / yard missing but under the cap / yard missing and discarding N EU/t) with the yard's bill read live; and the MATH panel scores the cap as its own weakest term with build the Switchyard as the fix rather than fit more Lasers.

More hatches will not break the ceiling. The cap is applied after the fitted-hatch ceiling — the injected figure is min(hatch ceiling, grid ceiling) — so it can only ever LOWER what is injected and can never push more into your hatches than they absorb. Adding Dynamo or Laser hatches raises the term the cap is already beating. Config flag switchyardSoftCap (default on) is the one setting that removes the mechanic.
The two process modules are the first and only things in this plant that consume EU, and they take it from the output manifold's four Energy Hatch sockets (y4). Those are still never a structure demand — the reactor and the turbine run perfectly without them, and un-forming a plant over furniture would arm the 20-minute decay-heat obligation — but with no hatch fitted neither module turns over, and the OUTPUT group says exactly that. The draw is per tick, not a lump sum at the end of a cycle: 409,600 EU in one payment is larger than an IV Energy Hatch's buffer, so a perfectly powered plant would otherwise stall forever.
Module sockets join no plant-wide hatch list. That is a correctness property: a shared list has no notion of which piece registered a hatch, so an Annex acid hatch in the plant's input-hatch list would satisfy the silo's feedwater demand — and a shared list is a shared inventory, so the reactor's rod distribution would eat the Annex's uraninite and the cascade's depleted rods would land in the Derrick's bus. Each socket keeps a dedicated field on the controller.
Projecting onto a lived-in platform. Every module block cell refuses to overwrite a standing tile entity: the cell is skipped and the module reports that it cannot form, rather than voiding a chest, a cable or an interface you had parked there. Clear the spot and project again.

In game, each module has its own questbook page — Blueprint: Vent Stack, Blueprint: Switchyard, Blueprint: Extraction Derrick, Blueprint: Enrichment Annex — plus The 100M Wall on the mastery row for the grid cap. All of their bills are generated from the same shape code as the table above. The 3D preview at docs/mockup/unit-one.html draws all four at their real footprints, at every tier.

3.8Turbine Hall Retired — museum piece

The original steam-to-EU module (MTE 28104, 5×4×9 at a 36..40, c 30..38) and its three turbine fans were retired by the Steam Dynamics ruling — replaced entirely by the rod-cascade → Spine → Spiral → Turbine Generator chain. Recipes are removed; existing halls and fans keep working as museum pieces until a later breaking release, and the Turbine Generator takes precedence when both exist. Its K/T/P fan-coolant-propellant multipliers live on in the new chain.

5Safety Systems

Core heat runs on one scale: passive decay is 2 heat/tick (doubled per Cooling Tower). Two thresholds matter, and two more failure modes live downstream.

EventTriggerConsequence
SCRAM heat ≥ 8,000 Critical shutdown — "core overheat — re-enable the reactor to restart". The campaign is lost; heat must fall before restart is sane.
MELTDOWN heat ≥ 10,000 Core destroyed: vertical detonation plus a lingering radiation field (30 minutes, centered at silo mid-depth behind the controller). Both honor config toggles. "The site is irradiated."
SEIZURE Coolant lost at speed on an already-hot chamber Losing coolant now TRIPS the fan (RPM ×0.95/tick) instead of cooking it, and the interlock stops an unsupplied plant from ever spinning up — but a chamber still gains ceil((RPM/1000)²)×3 friction heat/s while it spins down, so one that was already near SCRAM can seize: the Spine's four Turbine Housings are destroyed and the plant slams shut.
BLOWOUT Propellant on a non-TDA fan Legacy Turbine Hall failure — destroyed the hall's turbine fan. Museum-piece halls can still do it.

Decay heat — the shutdown obligation

Every shutdown (clean stop or SCRAM) starts a 20-minute decay-heat window (24,000 ticks) that demands 64 L/t of water. Starve it and heat climbs toward meltdown even with the reactor off.

  • Each Cooling Tower halves the decay water demand (floor 8 L/t) and doubles passive heat decay.
  • A Vent Stack halves the decay-heat window itself — 24,000 ticks (20 min) becomes 12,000 (10) — and halves the meltdown radiation field with it, 36,000 ticks (30 min) to 18,000 (15). Radius and dose are unchanged; the obligation simply ends sooner.
  • Two towers make post-shutdown cooling fully passive.
  • If the silo is dismantled mid-obligation, the debt is deferred (not waived) until the silo stands again.

Heat sources to watch

  • Residual Spine swirl: |S|² × 5 heat/s while running — the all-riding CW×4 pattern adds a constant 80 heat/s.
  • Missed injections: +50 heat per wasted litre, capped at 8 per slug (a dumped bucket cannot instantly destroy the plant — it "only" shoves it toward SCRAM).
  • Spiral starvation: the superlinear one — see §3.4. Speed squared, every tick.
  • Rotor shatter (stage dead until the rotor item is replaced) 0.17.0.
Dynamo discipline: the Turbine Generator clamps its EU to the summed dynamo capacity before injecting — GregTech detonates a multiblock whose dynamos are overfed. Size your dynamo hatches for the output you are chasing.

6Progression 0.22.0

Everything up to here is construction. From the moment the plant generates, it starts earning — four milestone lines climb just by running, and the points they pay buy a 25-node upgrade tree on the main station controller. Design of record: addon/docs/progression.md.

Nothing here can take anything away. Every unlock is a new maximum and every tree channel starts at a value that does nothing, so a plant sitting at zero progress derives a bit-identical EU figure to the one it derived in 0.21.0. There is no "was-already-X" grandfathering flag anywhere, because none was needed. Progress starts at zero on every plant the first time 0.22.0 loads — there is no back-fill.

6.1The four milestone lines

Each line watches one number the plant already knew about itself, and each has seven tiers. A line is at tier N once its counter has passed the Nth threshold — a plain table, no curve to reverse-engineer. No new tick loop was added: every counter rides a loop that already ran, and none of them is written by the structure check, which fires on every neighbour update.

LineCountsScored onWhat cannot farm it

The redline and the cycle gain always move together. OVERSPEED TRIM is opt-in. The tier raises the ceiling; it never moves the fan on its own. Chamber coolant draw goes with the square of RPM, so a plant plumbed for exactly 12,000 would have seen its steady draw rise 26 % at T5 and 56 % at T6, and the TC-7 interlock would then have throttled a plant that ran fine the day before. Type the redline you want into the RPM LIMIT box beside the rods box — default 12,000, clamped to what you have actually unlocked, exactly like the rods cap. The per-cycle gain is held to the same figure you authorise, so a plant on Lv 3 propellant (which settles at ≈9,227, under the redline) is not pushed to a higher equilibrium either. Every panel prints the redline in use and names the headroom you have not claimed.
Build Tier 6/7 is deliberately not in this system. Extending MAX_TIER means new silo layers, new column caps, and — because every attached building's offset derives from the silo edge — moving the Spine, Spiral, manifold and both Cooling Towers on the 50×50 pad. That cannot be verified without raising one in world, and un-forming a standing plant is the exact failure this release was built to avoid. The BURNUP ladder gives the same class of reward (+25 % pillar output at 30/24) through three clamps.

6.3The upgrade tree

Every tier also pays progression points, triangularly — 1+2+…+t, so a maxed line is worth 28 by itself and a full 28-tier sweep pays 112. Points are the tree's only currency, and the whole tree costs exactly 112: a maxed campaign buys all of it with nothing wasted and nothing short.

Open the main station controller — the Nuclear Plant Foundation itself, not a control block — and click the small chart button at the bottom right of its GUI, to the left of the two number fields. The tree opens on a panel beside it.

  • One free root (Unit One Commissioning, 0 points, no effect) and four branches, one per milestone line, six nodes deep. 25 nodes, 28 points a branch.
  • Prerequisites are derived, not typed: the node one step shallower on the same branch, plus — at depth 6 — the depth-4 node of the next branch round. Those four cross-links make it a graph rather than four ladders, and force breadth at the end of the tree. 24 spine edges + 4 cross-links = 28 edges, and the connector lines the panel draws come straight off them, so the picture cannot show an edge the rules do not enforce.
  • The gate is one sentence: a node at depth N on a branch needs that branch's milestone line at tier N. The branch that rewards your turbine is the branch the TURBINE line opens.
  • Left-click buys, right-click refunds. Refunds are free, unlimited and reversible; the one refusal is a refund that would orphan a node you still hold. Hover any node for its title, effect, cost, prerequisites, gate and which single requirement is currently failing — there is no second window.
  • Purchases are server-authoritative. The click carries a node index; the server re-asks ownership, prerequisites, gate and budget against its own record. The greying-out in the panel is decoration.

A node carries a channel and a positive delta, never code — so the set of things the tree can possibly affect is these twelve numbers and nothing else, and the effect text the panel prints is generated from the channel and the delta, so a node cannot advertise one thing and do another.

6.4What persists, and where

The campaign lives on the main station controller, in its own NBT — not on the player and not on the world. Four counters and a purchased-node set; nothing derived is ever stored, because tiers, multipliers, unlocks and point totals are pure functions of those five values and a stored copy would be a second source of truth.

SurvivesHow
a world reloadsaveNBTData → the one serializer, force = true
a chunk unloading and coming back
a wrenchsetItemNBT → the same serializer, force = false. The campaign rides along inside the dropped machine item and loads straight back when it is placed.
the structure un-forming and re-formingthe structure check never touches the record — and says so in its own comment, beside the identical exclusion for the two process modules' cycle counters.

One serializer, two writers, one reader — they cannot drift apart, because there is only one of each, and item NBT and tile NBT both land in the same loadNBTData. Nodes are keyed on disk by name, never by ordinal, so inserting a node in a later build can never silently reassign an unlock the player already owns. And force = false writes nothing at all for a controller that has never earned anything, so a fresh one still drops as a plain, stackable machine item.

Do not clear counters and expect the tree to follow. Load-time validation enforces prerequisites only — never the budget and never the milestone gates. Those are purchase-time rules, and enforcing them on load is precisely how a system would delete a campaign. Zero the counters under a bought tree and the plant simply reports negative points available and refuses further purchases; refunds keep working, so you can settle up.

Reading it, and testing it

A MILESTONES group closes the status report on the controller GUI, both control blocks and the handheld scanner — four line rows, a roll-up, the unlock list, then two upgrade-tree lines. All of it comes out of the same shared formatters, so no two readouts can describe different plants. It is ungated on the silo, like the tower and module lines: REPROCESSING accumulates on a bare platform's Derrick, and that panel is the only one a player still building has.

Milestones 12/28 · plant output x1.310 · 24/112 progression points earned Upgrades 9/25 · 21/112 points committed · 3 available Tree effects: plant output x1.10 · control-rod cap +2
Testing (op, permission level 2). Progress starts at zero by ruling, so two debug commands exist to reach a tier in minutes instead of weeks — run either beside the controller.

/btsmilestone — report · set <line> <n> · tier <line> <0-7> · all <0-7> · reset · verify
/btstree — report · buy <node> · refund <node> · all · reset · audit · verify

verify is the one that matters. It round-trips the record through both writers into fresh copies and diffs every counter and every node, so a forgotten key shows up on the tile path and the wrench path at once — without wrenching anything. audit re-sums the 25 node costs against 112 and prints every channel against its base. Both reports end by reading the live limits back off the plant rather than off the tables, so an unlock that failed to reach the code that uses it is visible immediately.

Nothing in the D-08 ladders was re-priced, and no rung got harder. The six legacy one-step Mixer recipes are kept standing, unchanged — they are what a running plant drinks, and retiring them would stop a working plant dead on jar swap. The new chains are added beside them as a cheaper route.
